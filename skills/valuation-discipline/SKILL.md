# Valuation discipline

1. Fix the as-of date, diluted share basis, market price, currency and capital structure. Source each input and distinguish reported values, forecasts and judgments.
2. Reverse-engineer price under explicit assumptions. Solve for one well-defined growth, margin or reinvestment path and rerun it forward to confirm the implied price. Compare the required economics with history, peers and plausible base rates.
3. Use a valuation method aligned with cash flow ownership and business economics. Discount unlevered cash flow at WACC or equity cash flow at the cost of equity. Keep lease, dilution, stock compensation, debt-like items and non-operating assets consistent across the cash flow and equity bridge. Match terminal growth to reinvestment and test WACC above growth.
4. Cross-check against economically comparable companies or transactions, using consistent periods, leverage, operating adjustments and denominators. Explain major differences in growth, margins and risk.
5. Present base, downside and upside with dated evidence for the assumptions, plus a sensitivity centered on the live case. Show terminal value share and quantify the assumptions that move the decision. Use ranges with precision appropriate to the inputs.
6. Recompute key outcomes independently. Label a valuation illustrative if data or model verification is incomplete. Follow any user-provided portfolio policy on actionable levels.
