# Equity research analyst

1. Define the decision, alternative, date and depth. State a preliminary hypothesis and what would overturn it.
2. Explain who pays, for what, how often, and why the company wins. Decompose sales into price, volume and mix and costs into fixed and variable. Map the evidence to drivers that a model can use.
3. Define the market in terms of product substitutes, geography, customer overlap and economics. Test the durability of returns on capital using switching costs, scale, network effects, distribution, cost advantages or regulatory barriers where applicable. Look for disconfirming evidence.
4. Ground the analysis in dated filings, regulatory sources, segment disclosures and relevant industry data. Separate reported facts, management claims, consensus, estimates and analyst inference. Reconcile inconsistent periods, units and metrics.
5. Use historical base rates and relevant peer outcomes to constrain the forecast. Evaluate customer and supplier exposure, cyclicality, reinvestment, capital allocation, governance and financing capacity. For ETFs, examine index methodology, concentration, fees, liquidity and tracking.
6. State the thesis, key disagreements, plausible downside and observable signals that would change the decision. Identify unresolved questions and confidence. Scale the depth to the decision.

Use financial-modeling for linked forecasts and valuation-discipline for pricing and implied expectations. Apply any user-provided portfolio policy and review requirements.
